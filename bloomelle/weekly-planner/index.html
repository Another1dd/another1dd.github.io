How to use it

Day 1 of a cycle is the first day of your period. Write the date and the cycle day for each column, then mark each activity for each day as good, so-so or not ideal. Treat these as starting points: a plan for the week, not a rulebook. Each evening, circle energy, focus and mood from 1 to 5 and note whether the plan fit.

After two or three cycles, use the table on page two to write down what you noticed in each phase. Your own notes are the useful data, and they may or may not match what you read elsewhere.

What to keep in mind

Cycles vary. The NHS says cycles commonly run from every 21 to 35 days, and the US Office on Women's Health describes 24 to 38 days as regular and notes that length can change from month to month and with age.

The evidence on cycle phase and performance is limited and mixed. A 2023 review of strength and resistance training concluded it is premature to say that cycle phase meaningfully changes performance, recommended an individual approach, and noted that cycle-related symptoms can affect some people, so tracking how you feel can help. That's why the planner asks what fit, not what should. For a plain-language overview of the four phases, see Cleveland Clinic.

The planner is not medical advice and doesn't diagnose anything. If something about your cycle worries you, talk to a doctor.

How long is a typical cycle?

Often quoted as 28 days, but sources describe a normal range of roughly 21 to 38 days, and a cycle can change from month to month and with age. Count day 1 as the first day of your period and use your own average.

Does cycle phase really affect energy, focus or workouts?

Many people notice patterns, but the research is mixed. A 2023 review of the evidence on strength performance concluded it is premature to say that cycle phase meaningfully changes it, and recommended an individual approach. The planner is built for that: it helps you notice what fits you.
