Common hot flash triggers

Everyone's trigger list looks a little different, but these come up the most:

Alcohol, especially red wine

Alcohol widens blood vessels near the skin (vasodilation), which produces that sudden flush of heat.

Caffeine

Stimulates the same adrenergic pathways involved in your body's temperature response — separate from any effect of hot liquid itself.

Hot beverages, even decaf

Tea, coffee, or soup can trigger a flash purely from temperature, independent of caffeine content.

Spicy food

Capsaicin activates heat-sensing receptors that overlap with the pathways involved in a hot flash.

High-sodium and MSG-heavy meals

Takeout, processed soups, and cured meats are often reported, likely tied to fluid shifts rather than heat directly.

Histamine-rich foods

Aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, and red wine can trigger a reaction separate from alcohol content alone.

Sugar and refined carbs

Less about heat directly, more about a blood sugar swing feeding into the same stress response.

Non-menopause causes

An overactive thyroid, anxiety, certain medications (niacin), and an ALDH2 alcohol sensitivity can all cause similar flushing on their own.

General information, not medical advice. If your pattern doesn't match what you'd expect, bring your logged data to a doctor rather than self-diagnosing from a list.
